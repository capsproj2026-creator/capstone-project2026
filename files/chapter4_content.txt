H1|CHAPTER 4
H1|METHODOLOGY, RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
P|This chapter presents the methodology, results, and discussion of the Integrated Smart Campus Vehicle Management System (ISCVMS) for Camarines Sur Polytechnic Colleges (CSPC). Following the Agile Development Methodology described in Chapter 3, the researchers organized the work into iterative phases: Plan, Design, Develop, Test, Deploy, and Review. Each phase reports the activities performed, the deliverables produced, and the Specific Objectives (SOs) achieved. Results linked to Specific Objectives are presented using the PIIILS structure (Purpose, Inputs, Implementation, Instruments, Learnings, and Summary of results). The narrative is grounded in the implemented system stack: Laravel 12 with MongoDB for the web application, ESP32 with RC522 RFID readers for boom-gate access control, Laravel Reverb for live gate monitoring, and a Python YOLOv9 service with OpenCV and EasyOCR for AI-powered parking occupancy and violation support.
H2|4.1 Plan
P|The Plan phase defined the product vision, stakeholder needs, and prioritized Product Backlog that guided subsequent Agile sprints. This phase primarily addressed Specific Objective 1: performing a thorough review of existing manual procedures at CSPC in order to define the necessary elements of the system along with the system's technical requirements.
H3|4.1.1 Stakeholder Engagement and Requirement Discovery
P|Using purposive sampling as stated in Chapter 3, the researchers engaged campus security personnel, General Services Unit (GSU) administrative staff, vehicle owners (students, faculty, and staff), and IT experts. Interviews and gate observations confirmed recurring operational themes: long dwell time during rush hours caused by sticker and logbook checks, incomplete or delayed entry records, limited visibility of parking occupancy, and difficulty enforcing parking and traffic rules without a centralized digital trail. Secondary sources such as daily vehicle logbooks, sticker registration forms, and gate incident notes further informed the backlog items.
H3|4.1.2 Product Backlog
P|The Product Backlog grouped epics that map directly to the modules in Specific Objective 2. High-priority items included role-based portals for Admin, Guard, and Vehicle Owner users; digital vehicle registration with approval workflow; RFID card assignment and gate scan authorization; live gate monitoring; AI parking occupancy updates; violation logging with evidence and three-strike enforcement; visitor temporary RFID support; and reporting exports. Non-functional backlog items covered authentication and authorization, API token protection for gate devices, realtime event broadcasting, and automated regression tests.

H3|PIIILS Result for Specific Objective 1
P|Purpose. To assess CSPC's manual vehicle monitoring process and translate findings into a clear Product Backlog and technical requirements for ISCVMS.
P|Inputs. Interview and observation notes from security and GSU stakeholders; sample logbook and sticker processes; related literature and systems reviewed in Chapter 2; campus operational constraints (gates, parking areas, staffing).
P|Implementation. The researchers conducted requirement workshops, thematic analysis of pain points (dwell time, incomplete logs, parking search, weak enforcement), and sprint planning that ranked RFID access, digital registration, AI parking, and violation tracking as must-have capabilities.

P|Learnings. Manual sticker and logbook workflows are the primary bottlenecks during peak hours; stakeholders need role-separated dashboards rather than a single generic interface; hardware integration (ESP32 RFID and camera AI) must be treated as first-class backlog items, not afterthoughts.
P|Summary of results. Specific Objective 1 was achieved in the Plan phase through a documented requirement set and prioritized Product Backlog (Table 15) that became the basis for Design and Develop sprints.
H2|4.2 Design
P|The Design phase produced the system architecture, data models, interface wireframes for Admin/Guard/User portals, and integration contracts between Laravel and the ESP32/AI services. Design decisions follow the Model-View-Controller pattern of Laravel, with service-layer classes for RFID access, gate hardware commands, AI occupancy, and violation enforcement.
H3|4.2.1 System Architecture
P|ISCVMS uses a multi-tier architecture. The client layer includes web browsers for administrators, guards, and vehicle owners, plus IoT edge devices (ESP32 RFID gate controllers and IP cameras). The application layer hosts the Laravel 12 backend (routing, authentication, business rules, APIs) and the Python AI parking service (YOLOv9 inference, zone occupancy, optional plate OCR). Realtime gate events are broadcast through Laravel Reverb to the Guard Live Gate monitor. The data layer uses MongoDB collections for users, vehicles, gate logs, parking areas/slots, violation logs, and related configuration documents. Hardware communicates with Laravel through authenticated HTTP endpoints such as POST /api/rfid/scan and POST /api/rfid/heartbeat, and AI occupancy/violation ingest endpoints under the AI parking API controllers.
H3|4.2.2 Module and Interface Design
P|Five core modules were specified: (1) User Management with role-permission middleware for Admin, Guard, and User portals; (2) Vehicle Registration with document submission, admin approval/decline, and RFID assignment; (3) RFID Gate Access with entry/exit scan decisions, shared boom actuation for exit grants, emergency open, and gate logs; (4) AI-Powered Parking Monitoring with calibrated parking zones, occupancy updates, and rule-based overtime/wrong-slot/unauthorized events; and (5) Violation Tracking with manual and AI-assisted logging, evidence photos, notifications, and three-strike auto-lock logic. Supporting designs cover visitor temporary RFID, plate lookup, and report exports (CSV/PDF/Excel).

H2|4.3 Develop
P|The Develop phase converted the backlog and design into a working Integrated Smart Campus Vehicle Management System. This phase primarily addressed Specific Objective 2: developing a web-based system that provides User Management, Vehicle Registration, RFID Gate Access, AI-Powered Parking Monitoring, and Violation Tracking modules.
H3|4.3.1 User Management Module
P|Administrators can create and manage accounts, assign roles, and configure permissions. Guards and vehicle owners access role-scoped routes under /admin, /guard, and /user prefixes. Authentication flows include login, registration, and email verification. Suspensions and lock states are enforced so that restricted accounts cannot obtain gate access. Seeded demo accounts support local demonstration and training.
H3|4.3.2 Vehicle Registration Module
P|Vehicle owners submit registration details and supporting documents through the public registration flow. Administrators approve or decline applications; approval notifications inform owners, and RFID cards can be assigned through the admin RFID management screens. Only approved and granted vehicles are eligible for automated gate entry.
H3|4.3.3 RFID Gate Access Module
P|ESP32 firmware for entry and exit gates reads RC522 tags and posts scan payloads to Laravel. RfidAccessService evaluates card registration, vehicle grant status, lock state, and entry/exit context, then returns grant or deny. Successful grants can actuate the boom (servo or relay). Heartbeat endpoints allow the entry controller to execute queued open commands, including shared-boom behavior for exit grants and emergency open for guards. GateScanProcessed events update the Guard Live Gate interface in realtime. Access attempts are persisted as GateLog records for audit and reporting.
H3|4.3.4 AI-Powered Parking Monitoring Module
P|The Python AI parking service consumes camera streams, runs YOLOv9 vehicle detection, and maps detections to calibrated parking zones using geometry and Intersection-over-Union style occupancy logic. Occupancy updates are posted to Laravel services that refresh ParkingArea and ParkingSlot status for Admin, Guard, and User parking views. Optional EasyOCR plate recognition supports plate-to-owner lookup. Rule evaluation can generate overtime, wrong parking, or unauthorized parking events for downstream violation handling.
H3|4.3.5 Violation Tracking Module
P|Guards and administrators can record violations manually; AI parking rules can also contribute violation events with evidence. ViolationEnforcementService applies sanction logic, including a three-strike path that can lock vehicle access. Notifications and mail alerts inform stakeholders. Evidence handling keeps sensitive files protected while still supporting adjudication and reporting.

H2|4.4 Test
P|The Test phase verified that ISCVMS behaves correctly against intended inputs and outputs without relying on white-box inspection of every internal line of code. Testing followed Chapter 3's Black Box Testing (BBT) plan and ISO/IEC 25010 quality characteristics: Functionality, Usability, Reliability, Security, and Performance. Automated PHPUnit feature tests in the project repository provided repeatable evidence for RFID API behavior, AI occupancy ingest, role security, visitor RFID, and violation evidence flows. Stakeholder Likert evaluation against ISO/IEC 25010 is prepared in this section as structured placeholders to be completed after formal respondent evaluation.

H2|4.5 Deploy
P|The Deploy phase focused on making ISCVMS runnable for demonstration and pilot use within the CSPC project context, addressing Specific Objective 4: implement and deploy the system within Camarines Sur Polytechnic Colleges (initially within scoped parking and gate pilot areas).
H3|4.5.1 Software Deployment
P|The web application is deployed locally using the project start scripts (start.bat / start.ps1), which bring up Laravel, Vite assets when needed, Laravel Reverb for realtime gate updates, and optionally the AI parking service window. Environment configuration uses .env for MongoDB connection, RFID API tokens, Reverb settings, and AI camera endpoints. Database seeders provide baseline roles, demo users, and sample AI test lot data for walkthroughs.
H3|4.5.2 Hardware and Edge Deployment
P|ESP32 entry and exit firmware is configured through rfid_gate_config.h (Wi-Fi, server base URL, gate IDs, actuator mode). RC522 readers are wired per the hardware README; entry actuators use servo or relay on the designated GPIO, with LED and buzzer feedback. The AI parking service is configured for RTSP or local camera sources, zone calibration files, and Laravel callback URLs. Deployment remains limited to designated pilot gates and parking zones, consistent with the scope and limitations in Chapter 1.

H2|4.6 Review
P|The Review phase closed each Agile iteration by comparing sprint outcomes with backlog goals and stakeholder expectations. Feedback from security and GSU walkthroughs, together with failing-then-fixed automated tests, informed backlog refinement for subsequent sprints.
H3|4.6.1 Sprint Review Outcomes
P|Reviews confirmed that role-separated portals reduced confusion compared with a single dashboard concept. Gate monitoring benefited from realtime events, but reviewers emphasized clear grant/deny messaging and reliable boom feedback. AI parking reviews highlighted the need for careful zone calibration and transparency that occupancy is vision-based and may degrade under poor lighting. Violation reviews affirmed the usefulness of three-strike enforcement while requesting clear owner notifications and evidence handling.
H3|4.6.2 Backlog Refinements
P|Refinements carried into later sprints included strengthening RFID token header authentication, shared-boom exit coordination via heartbeat commands, visitor RFID expiration rules, plate lookup assist for guards, richer reporting exports, and additional automated tests for security and occupancy edge cases. Remaining review actions include completing ISO/IEC 25010 field evaluation (Tables 19-24) and capturing campus pilot performance metrics under real peak traffic.
P|Overall, Chapters 1 to 3 established the need, related work, and technical plan; Chapter 4 shows that Agile execution produced a coherent ISCVMS implementation with verified critical behaviors and a clear path to finalize stakeholder evaluation scores.
